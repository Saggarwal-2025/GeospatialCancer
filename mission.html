Mission

Research clearly. Communicate responsibly. Advocate constructively.

We are a student-driven legislative advocacy and data analysis project focused on understanding possible relationships among cancer patterns, environmental data, and industrial activity across Texas.

Who We Are

A student-led research and advocacy project

Our work brings together computer programming, cancer statistics, social research, environmental information, and civic engagement. The goal is not to begin with a conclusion, but to identify patterns and questions that deserve closer study.

What We Do

Investigate possible environmental relationships

We review industrial sites, public environmental records, cancer data, and related information to examine where possible associations may warrant further research. We distinguish observed patterns from confirmed causation.

Why It Matters

Make complex information easier to understand

Communities and decision-makers can face technical data that is difficult to interpret. We aim to translate research into accessible explanations without overstating what the evidence proves.

Our Advocacy Role

Connect research with civic action

We research relevant local, state, and federal representatives, prepare findings for outreach, and pursue interviews or conversations that can raise awareness and encourage further investigation or appropriate action.

Core standard: A correlation, geographic overlap, or project hypothesis is not presented as proof that pollution caused a cancer outcome.

Three Principles

What guides the project

01

Investigate the Data

Use cancer statistics, environmental records, programming, and public information to identify patterns that deserve closer review.

02

Make Research Understandable

Translate technical information into clear explanations that students, communities, and public officials can understand.

03

Advocate for Action

Share findings with relevant offices and stakeholders while clearly separating observed patterns from claims of proven causation.
